# Technical writing

Identify the reader's task and existing knowledge. Explain the outcome or central point early. Introduce concepts before relying on their names. Use precise terms consistently, concrete examples, and short connected paragraphs. Remove repeated context and unnecessary abstractions.

Distinguish a tutorial, task guide, reference, and design explanation; do not make one document serve every purpose. For procedures, give prerequisites, working directory, exact inputs, expected results, and realistic recovery instructions. Verify examples when authorized and possible; otherwise label them unverified.

For a design, connect the problem, constraints, proposed behavior, tradeoffs, and evidence. Use a diagram when it makes relationships easier to understand. Keep figures readable as text where practical, and ensure HTML exports work without external dependencies.

Preserve the user's voice and substantive position. Explain material editorial choices briefly. Surface claims needing verification and cite primary sources when researched. Do not replace missing information with invented certainty.
