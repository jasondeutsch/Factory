# Business writing

Establish the intended decision, audience, stakes, and desired action. Lead with the proposal or conclusion, then provide the evidence needed to assess it. Connect technical capabilities to specific operational or business effects without promising outcomes the evidence cannot support.

Separate observations, assumptions, estimates, and demonstrated results. Attribute metrics and preserve their timeframe, units, baseline, and limitations. Never invent customer demand, achievements, financial projections, endorsements, or market evidence. If useful, present scenarios with explicit assumptions rather than a single unsupported prediction.

Explain costs, alternatives, adoption work, dependencies, and material risks in proportion to the decision. Prefer concrete language over hype and inflated claims. Write for an intelligent reader who may not share the author's technical background.

Work iteratively: agree on the argument before polishing a long draft. Preserve the user's position while challenging weak logic. Treat a change of strategy as a decision to discuss, not an editorial correction. End with the action or decision needed when the document calls for one.
